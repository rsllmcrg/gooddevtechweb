## The problem

Students already have the material. It's in a notebook, on a whiteboard photo,
or on a printed handout. What they don't have is a quick way to turn it into
something they can test themselves on. Flashcard apps start from a blank card,
so the notes end up in one place and the cards in another, and the student
spends the evening switching between a camera roll and a keyboard.

Quizzing also tends to be all or nothing. You work through the whole deck
again, including the cards you already know, when the real work is the two or
three you keep getting wrong.

## The approach

The app is built around one loop: **photo, cards, study, quiz**. Each step is
one screen and one tap away from the next.

- **Start from the notes.** A new deck takes a name and, optionally, a photo,
  either taken on the spot with the camera or picked from the library. The
  photo is pinned to the top of the deck.
- **Write cards with the source in view.** The add-card form sits right under
  the photo. Tap the photo to enlarge it and read the handwriting, then tap
  again to shrink it and keep typing. Cards can be edited or deleted in place.
- **Study at your own pace.** Study mode shows one card at a time: tap to flip
  between question and answer, step through with Prev and Next, or shuffle the
  deck so you don't just memorise the order.
- **Quiz yourself honestly.** Quiz mode asks a question, reveals the answer on
  request, and lets the student mark it "Got it" or "Missed it". A progress bar
  and a running score keep track as they go.
- **Review only what you missed.** The results screen gives a percentage,
  lists the missed cards with their answers, and offers "Retry Missed" to
  quiz on just those, or a full retake.
- **Private by default.** Decks, photos, and cards are stored on the device.
  There is no account to create and nothing to sync before you can start.

One Expo codebase, with file-based routing through Expo Router, ships to iOS,
Android, and the web, so the same app runs on a student's phone and on a
laptop browser.

## The outcome

A student can go from a page of notes to a working quiz in a couple of
minutes, without leaving the app. Study sessions focus on the cards that
need work, and because everything stays on the device, there is nothing to
sign up for before the first deck.

The project also shows how we build mobile apps: one React Native codebase
that ships natively and on the web, with the platform's camera and photo
library built in rather than added on later.
