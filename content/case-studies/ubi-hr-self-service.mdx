## The problem

A university HR office spends most of its day answering the same handful of
questions. How many leave days do I have left? How do I file? When does my
contract end? What do I need for a Certificate of Employment? Each answer is
already written down somewhere — in the employee's 201 file or in the
handbook — but finding it means a trip to the HRMO window or a message that
waits until office hours.

The expensive problems are the ones nobody asks about. A faculty member's PRC
license lapses and they can't be assigned board-program subjects. Vacation
credits past the carry-over limit quietly expire on December 31. A
probationary contract nears its end with no one reminded. By the time HR finds
out, it's already too late to fix.

## The approach

UBi is a chat-first self-service assistant for faculty and staff, with an
administration dashboard behind it for HR.

- **Answers from the record, not from a guess.** Personal questions — leave
  balances, contract status, credentials — are answered from the employee's own
  201 file. Policy questions are answered only from an HR-curated knowledge
  base, and every policy answer names its source document and section.
- **Speaks the way people actually ask.** Employees write in English, Tagalog,
  or a mix of both. UBi understands all three and answers in kind; policy
  keywords include the Tagalog phrasing people really type.
- **Files requests in conversation.** "Sick leave, October 24–25" becomes a
  leave application. UBi counts the working days, checks the remaining
  balance, and asks for an explicit confirmation before anything is submitted.
  Certificate of Employment and service record requests work the same way, and
  employees can check the status of their requests in chat.
- **Warns before things lapse.** A daily job watches professional licenses,
  contract end dates, and leave credits that will be forfeited, and alerts
  each employee starting 90 days out. HR sees who was reminded and whether
  they've read it.
- **Stays in its lane.** Off-topic questions get a polite redirect to what UBi
  can actually help with, in the language they were asked in.
- **Gives HR one place to work.** The admin dashboard tracks daily query
  volume, the request approval queue (with remarks, through to "ready for
  release"), feedback and concern reports with threaded replies, employee
  records, and the expiry monitor. It also reports a deflection rate: how many
  questions were resolved without an HR officer stepping in.
- **Policies are data, not code.** HR adds or edits a policy — title,
  keywords, source document, and whether it applies to everyone or only
  faculty — and the assistant's very next answer reflects it.

Access is role-based: faculty and staff see their own records and the chat, and
HR administrators get the dashboard.

## The outcome

Faculty and staff get answers at any hour, in their own words, backed by their
own records and the official policy text. Leave filing, COE requests, and status
checks no longer need a trip to the HRMO window. HR spends its time on the
approval queue and the cases that genuinely need a person, and finds out about
expiring licenses and contracts months ahead instead of after the fact.
